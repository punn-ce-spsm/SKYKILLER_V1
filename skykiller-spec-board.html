This is the demo-scale build of a counter-UAS system on the SkyLock pattern: many sensors feeding one fused air picture, an operator console that owns the decision, and a graduated ladder of effects. The whole detect–track–identify chain is real and runs on real hardware. The effect stage is simulated end to end — the system computes, displays, and logs an engagement, and transmits nothing.

That split is not a shortcut, it is the design. Jamming and GNSS interference are unlawful without specific authority almost everywhere, and the interesting engineering — multi-sensor fusion, track continuity, classification, the rules-of-engagement gate — all lives upstream of the transmitter. Building the sensing and decision half honestly gets you a system that is legal to run on a Saturday in a field, and that has a real transmitter-shaped hole waiting for it if authority is ever granted.

Every number on this sheet is a demo-scale estimate for the specific parts listed in §8, not a vendor specification. Ranges in particular vary by a factor of three with antenna, terrain, and ambient RF.

1

Scope — what is real, what is simulated, what is never run

truth table

Read this before anything else on the sheet. A counter-drone demo that blurs these columns is a liability; one that states them plainly can be shown to anyone.

Real

Built, runs on hardware, works on an uncooperative target

  • RF energy detection and coarse bearing on 2.4 / 5 GHz — passive spectral scan
  • Remote ID decode (Wi-Fi NAN + Bluetooth 5 Long Range)
  • EO/IR detection, classification and visual track
  • Acoustic rotor-signature detection and bearing
  • Cross-sensor association and fusion into one track
  • Kinematic tracking — UKF in a local ENU frame
  • Threat scoring against declared zones
  • Pan-tilt slew-to-cue driven by the fused picture
  • Operator console, ROE state machine, audit log
  • CoT export to a TAK server / ATAK end user device

Simulated

Real message contracts and real timing, synthetic outcome

  • All three effect tiers — issued, timed, logged, never radiated
  • Radar plots, injected onto the live bus by a track generator
  • The target’s post-effect behaviour (return-to-home, descent)
  • The net interceptor’s intercept solution and flight path
  • Effect-assessment feedback — “did the target respond?”

Never run

Not built, and not reachable from any code path in the system

  • Any transmission on a drone control band
  • Any GNSS spoofing or GNSS denial
  • Kinetic launch against an aircraft that has not consented
  • Any effect issued without a logged human authorisation
  • Operation against a third party’s aircraft
The guarantee, stated precisely. No wideband transmitter and no RF amplifier is in the build. The SDRs are receive-only silicon. The three parts that can transmit — two Wi-Fi cards and an ESP32 — are commodity, low-power, narrowband radios run strictly in passive modes: monitor, NAN listen, BLE scan. None of them can produce the wideband power a denial effect requires, and no effect code path touches a radio at all. So the honest claim is not “nothing here can radiate” — it is that nothing here can perform T1 or T2, by construction rather than by discipline.
2

System architecture

fig. 1

Four bands, left to right, in the order a photon or a radio burst travels through them: sense, fuse, decide, effect. Every sensor lane publishes the same Detection message onto one bus and knows nothing about the lanes beside it, which is what lets the radar slot be filled later — or filled today by a simulator — without touching anything downstream.

Why the bus matters more than any single sensor. Each lane below is individually mediocre and individually defeatable — and each is blind to a band or a condition the others cover. That is the honest position at this budget. The value is in the union: an attacker who goes radio-silent to beat L1 still makes rotor noise for L3, and an attacker who flies at night to beat L2 still has to emit or be heard. Fusion is what turns four weak sensors into one that is hard to walk past.

Remote ID is the highest-value lane per dollar, and the easiest to over-trust. Fifty dollars of receiver hands you a cooperative aircraft’s serial number, its position, and where its pilot is standing — a capability that costs six figures to approximate any other way. It is also purely a courtesy protocol: it tells you about every drone except the one flown by someone who does not want to be told about. Treat an L1b identity as strong evidence and its absence as no evidence at all.

Kill chain and latency budget

fig. 2

Drawn to scale on a single time axis, because the scale is the argument. The machine half of the chain fits in about a third of a second; the human decision is five to fifteen times longer than everything else combined. That is not a performance problem to be optimised away — it is the safety property the whole system exists to protect.

5

Effector ladder

fig. 3

Three tiers of escalating force, each with a real message contract, a real timing model, and a simulated outcome. The demonstrator executes the ladder in full — arms, authorises, escalates, logs — and radiates nothing at any tier.

The inversion in that last column is the real finding. Tier 1 is a weekend of software and a $300 transmitter, and firing it once is a federal offence in most of the world. Tier 3 is a hard robotics problem — and it is the tier a private builder can lawfully develop, test and fly, and the only one that touches an autonomous target with no radio link to break. If this project ever grows past a demonstrator, the effect work belongs in T3, and T1–T2 stay simulated permanently unless someone arrives with an authority document.

Operator console

fig. 4

The console is where the two seconds of §4 get spent, so it is designed around one question: can a person decide correctly, from a cold start, in the time the track gives them? Everything on the threat card exists to answer that — what it is, how sure we are, how it got that sure, where its pilot is standing, and how long until it matters.

Build plan

13 weeks · P0–P5

Each phase ends on a single falsifiable test. If the exit criterion does not pass, the phase is not done — the failure mode this schedule is built to avoid is four half-working sensor lanes and no fused track.

P0wk 1–2

Bench

Compute node, MQTT broker, message schemas, one SDR on a desk. No mast, no field, no console — just the spine everything else plugs into.

Exit criterionA real drone control-link burst, transmitted across the room, produces a well-formed timestamped Detection on the bus.
P1wk 3–4

Lane 1 complete

Both halves of L1: RF energy detection with two-antenna bearing, and full Remote ID decode over Wi-Fi NAN and Bluetooth 5 Long Range.

Exit criterionA target powered up at 200 m yields its serial, its position and its operator’s position on the bus within 3 s of spin-up.
P2wk 5–7

Fusion and console

Association, the UKF tracker in local ENU, threat scoring against zones, and the first working operator console. The radar simulator comes up here, because a second synthetic lane is the cheapest way to prove the fusion logic is not secretly single-sensor.

Exit criterionOne drone flying a 200 m orbit holds a single continuous track ID for 90 s, with zero track breaks and zero duplicate tracks.
P3wk 8–10

Cue and classify

Camera lane with slew-to-cue off the fused bearing, CV detection and classification, and the acoustic lane. This is the hardest phase and the one most likely to slip; the pan-tilt calibration alone is a week.

Exit criterionAn L1 bearing slews the camera and the CV lane confirms the same object within 4 s — and, separately, the acoustic lane alone holds a track with the target’s radio switched off.
P4wk 11–12

Effect ladder and interop

The ROE state machine, the three simulated effect tiers with real timing and escalation, the append-only audit log, and CoT export to a TAK server.

Exit criterionAn operator ARMs, authorises T1, and the request lands in the audit log with full sensor provenance — nothing radiates, and the same track appears as a CoT marker in ATAK on a second machine.
P5wk 13

Demonstration

The scripted eight-minute run of §7, plus the radio-silent repeat of leg 1 that shows where the system is weak.

Exit criterionTwo consecutive cold runs — ingress, detect, confirm, classify, authorise, simulated effect — with no operator action outside the console and no restarts.
P6optional

Hard-kill, if it is ever worth it

The net interceptor: airframe, launcher, and closing the loop from the fused track to a flown MAVLink intercept. A separate project in scale, and the only effector this build could lawfully fire.

Exit criterionA net intercept of a consenting, instrumented target over a cleared impact area, with a safety pilot able to abort at any point.

Standing constraints

read before every run

These are not disclaimers appended to the end of the sheet; they are the constraints the architecture was drawn around, which is why the transmitter-shaped hole in §2 is empty.

No effect-capable transmitter exists

The SDRs are receive-only silicon. The Wi-Fi cards and the ESP32 are transmit-capable radios, but they are narrowband, low-power, and run only in passive receive modes — none can produce a denial effect, and no effect code path opens a radio. Keep it that way: if a wideband transmit-capable SDR (HackRF, Pluto, bladeRF) or any RF amplifier ever enters the build, this entire document stops applying.

Jamming and GNSS interference

Unlawful without specific authority in effectively every jurisdiction. In the United States, counter-UAS mitigation authority sits with a short statutory list of federal agencies and does not extend to private parties or most law enforcement. Check your own jurisdiction before assuming anything here transfers; assume it does not.

Reception is not interception

Listening to Remote ID is receiving a beacon a drone broadcasts deliberately. Passively noting that energy exists in a band is generally fine too. Decoding the content of someone’s control or video link is a different act with a different legal character — the build detects and characterises signals, it does not demodulate payloads.

Remote ID captures personal data

An L1b hit contains a pilot’s live physical location. Treat those records the way you would treat any location data about an identifiable person: retain them for the run, then discard. Do not publish console screenshots containing a real operator position — the one in FIG. 4 is invented.

Flight operations

Private land with written permission, a safety pilot on the sticks for the whole run, visual line of sight at all times, airspace checked on the day, and a declared safe box. The target is your own aircraft and no one else’s — pointing any part of this at a third party’s drone is out of scope at every phase.

Say what it is

When showing this, lead with §1. A demonstrator described honestly as detect-and-decide with simulated effects is genuinely impressive engineering. The same demonstrator described as a working counter-drone weapon is a claim you cannot support and should not make.
