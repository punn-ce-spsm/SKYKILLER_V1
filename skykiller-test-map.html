TIER 1

The webcam bench — today, indoors, no drone

~40 min · nothing to buy

The visual lane tracks whatever you tell it to, so it can be verified against a person, a bottle or a cup. Four checks. The third one is the one that matters — the other three confirm the machinery works, and that one sets a number every later measurement is scaled by.

Setup, once

5 min
cd ~/Desktop/SKYKILLER_V1
python3.12 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt

Python 3.12, not 3.14 — torch has no 3.14 wheels and python3 on this Mac is 3.14. Re-run the install even if you have the venv already: scipy was added on 31 Aug for the association solver.

macOS will block the camera the first time and say nothing. If no window appears, open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera, enable your terminal app, then restart the terminal. Nothing in the code can grant this.
.venv/bin/python -m skykiller --config configs/hometest.yaml

A window opens; hold up a cup, a bottle or your phone. First run downloads a 5 MB model. Press q to quit. Detections also stream to the terminal as JSON.

Check 1 — does the track id hold?

5 min
Do
Stand in frame and walk slowly left to right.
Pass
One box follows you and the T- number stays the same for the whole crossing. Expect roughly 13 fps on your M5.
Fails if
A new id every few frames — the tracker is dropping you. Move more slowly, improve the lighting, or raise model.conf to cut false detections that steal the association.
Proves
Track-id stability, which is the single thing this whole lane exists to produce. Everything downstream keys off that number.

Check 2 — is the bearing maths right?

5 min
Do
Hold an object at the far left of frame, then the centre, then the far right, pausing at each. Watch the az readout.
Pass
The numbers sweep smoothly and are roughly symmetric about the centre. el goes positive when you raise the object, negative when you lower it.
Proves
The pixel→bearing conversion and its sign conventions. Not its accuracy — that is check 3.
do this one first

Check 3 — measure the real field of view

10 min

The config ships with a guessed 65°. Every azimuth, every elevation, every triangulated position and every aiming solution is scaled by that number. Replacing the guess with a measurement is the highest-value ten minutes available anywhere in this project, and it needs a tape measure and a sheet of paper.

1 · set up
Put a flat object of known width square-on to the camera at a measured distance. A sheet of A4 landscape is 0.297 m; a 330 ml can is 0.066 m. 2 m is a convenient distance.
2 · record
.venv/bin/python -m skykiller --config configs/hometest.yaml \
    --no-show --jsonl ~/Desktop/fov.jsonl
Hold it still for a few seconds, then Ctrl-C.
3 · read
The box width is the third number in bbox_xywh: 
tail -5 ~/Desktop/fov.jsonl | python3 -c "import sys,json;[print(json.loads(l)['extra']['bbox_xywh'][2]) for l in sys.stdin]"
4 · solve
.venv/bin/python -m skykiller calibrate \
    --width-px 1280 --object-px <the number above> \
    --object-m 0.297 --distance-m 2.0
Then
Put the printed value into camera.hfov_deg in both configs/l2.yaml and configs/hometest.yaml, and record it in MEMORY.md — it is listed there as unmeasured.

Check 4 — does range estimation work?

5 min

Only meaningful after check 3, because range is derived from the field of view.

.venv/bin/python -m skykiller --config configs/hometest.yaml --target-width-m 0.066
Pass
The r ~Xm on the box reads within about 10% of your tape measure.
Fails if
Wrong by a consistent factor → the field of view is still off, redo check 3. If r is absent, --target-width-m was not set — that is deliberate: a range derived from an assumed object size is a guess, and the system reports nothing rather than a confident wrong number.

Optional — the identity filter

15 min

Enrol a photo of yourself and the lane emits a Detection only for you. This is the same gate lane L1b will use for Remote ID serials — faces are just the implementation that can be tested indoors, and the lane code does not change when serials replace them.

.venv/bin/python -m skykiller enroll --image me.jpg --name me
.venv/bin/python -m skykiller --config configs/face.yaml
Pass
A green box on you labelled T-<id> me 0.9x, JSON in the terminal only while you are in frame. Someone else gets a thin grey not-target box and no JSON. The HUD reads tracks 2 emitting 1. Turn your head away — the box stays green, because identity belongs to the track, not the frame.
Numbers
Measured separation on a reference pair: 0.91–0.95 for the enrolled face against 0.07–0.08 for a different person, with the threshold at 0.363 — wide margins either side.
Careful
Two different thresholds live in configs/face.yaml. detect_threshold (0.5) decides whether something is a face; threshold (0.363) decides whether two faces are the same person. Do not lower the second because the first is failing.
The enrolment file is biometric data. models/identity/me.npy stays on this machine, models/ is gitignored, and photos are default-denied in .gitignore. Delete the file to revoke it. If you demo this to anyone, enrol them only with their say-so.
What tier 1 proves: detection, tracking, track-id stability, the bearing maths, the range maths, the message contract, both output sinks, the viewer, and your real frame rate.  What it cannot prove: that the drone detector finds a real drone at range. A close, large object never exercises small-target detection — which is the hard part, and the thing that actually fails in the field.
TIER 2

The Remote ID scan — today, outdoors, no flight

10 min · free · decides $50

Lane L1b hands you the drone's serial, its position and the operator's position, from a passive beacon. It is the highest-value capability in the demonstrator and it is also $50 of hardware you should not buy if your aircraft does not broadcast. Thailand has no FAA-style broadcast mandate, so this is genuinely uncertain — and a phone answers it.

The procedure

10 min
1
Install a scanner on an Android phone with Bluetooth 5 — OpenDroneID Receiver or Drone Scanner, both free. iPhone will not work — Apple restricts the Wi-Fi NAN access this needs.
2
Put the phone within ~30 m of the drone, in the open.
3
Power the drone on and let it acquire GPS. Do not fly — this needs no flight and therefore no registration.
4
Watch for a detection for two minutes.
OutcomeWhat it meansWhat you do
Detected L1b is go. Note the serial and whether it reported an operator position. Buy items 3 and 4 on the shopping list.
Nothing Check the drone's settings for a Remote ID toggle and its firmware version, then retry once. Do not buy items 3 and 4. Record the model and firmware; L1b gets re-planned around it.
Either way, write down the exact model. The whole RF lane is specced on DJI OcuSync at 2.4 / 5.8 GHz. An older or enterprise aircraft could differ — some DJI enterprise links use 1.4 GHz — and the antenna choice changes with the band.
Standing rule. Remote ID captures a real person's live location. Retain the logs for a run, then delete them, and never publish a console screenshot containing a real operator position.
GATE

Paperwork — start it now, it runs in parallel

weeks of lead time

Nothing in tier 3 can happen without all four of these, and none of them is work you can compress once you need them. They are the reason to start today rather than when the hardware arrives.

CAAT + NBTC registration

Flying an unregistered drone in Thailand is an offence. Registration is required with the Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand and, for an aircraft carrying a camera or radio transmitter, with the NBTC — normally both, regardless of weight, when a camera is fitted.

1
Start at caat.or.th and follow the UAV / drone registration path. It is done online.
2
Have ready: your ID or passport, the drone's make, model and serial number, and photographs of the aircraft.
3
Complete the radio-equipment registration with the NBTC — CAAT's portal links to it, or apply via nbtc.go.th.
4
Expect third-party liability insurance to be a condition.
Record
Registration numbers and expiry dates, in MEMORY.md.

Regulations change. Treat this as a starting point and confirm the current requirements on the CAAT site rather than on this page.

A site, and a second pair of hands

  • Private land, with written permission. A text message saying yes is enough — keep it.
  • Confirmed outside restricted airspace — before each run, not once. CAAT publishes no-fly zones; there are also 9 km exclusion radii around airports and restrictions near government and royal sites.
  • Roughly 600 × 600 m, with clear line of sight from the mast position for a ~300 m detection ring.
  • A safety pilot — a second person on the sticks for the whole run whose only job is to fly and abort. Not you: you will be at the console.
Record in MEMORY.md: site coordinates, owner contact, and the date permission was given.
TIER 3

The field tests — where the claims are actually settled

blocked by the gate

These four are the ones that can come back negative. Everything above either already passed or tests machinery; these test the product, and each one is written so that it can fail.

TestWhat you doPasses ifWhy it can fail
Track continuity
the build-1 exit criterion		 Hover the Mini 4 Pro at 15–20 m and hold it for 30 s. One track id for the full 30 s. The detector is a placeholder trained on ~1,000 images. A dropped frame restarts the id.
Small target at range
never tested, at all		 Walk the drone out in 25 m steps and record the range at which detection stops. Detection holds to the optical limit the lens predicts. This is the hard part of the whole problem and the placeholder detector has never seen it. Measured optical ceilings: webcam 26 m, 25 mm lens 128 m, 100 mm 601 m.
Bearing accuracy
σ is assumed, everywhere		 Fly a surveyed path and compare reported azimuth against truth. Error near the 0.5° the whole system assumes. Every covariance, every gate and every aiming solution is scaled by this. If it is 2°, the accuracy claims move with it.
Two posts, real terrain
the product claim		 Run the demonstration for real: a ground camera behind a treeline against an elevated observer, same target, same trajectory. The elevated pair sees the target materially earlier. The masking model is a crest-line screen, not a terrain model. Real vegetation is partial, and the whole case rests on line of sight.
The frame-rate question you cannot answer indoors. The drone detector runs at 4.7 fps (213 ms/frame, YOLO11x at imgsz 1280 on MPS). That is adequate for a hovering or walking-pace target and marginal above ~10 m/s. Whether it holds a track on a real drone crossing at speed is untested — the synthetic clip moved slowly.

KNOWN

Limits no test on this list will find

documented, not defects

These are decided, written down and in some cases deliberately tested for, so nobody discovers them in front of a customer. Do not spend field time looking for them.

  • A hostile inside a friendly's correlation gate reads FRIENDLY. Inherent to position-correlation IFF. Closing it needs Remote ID serials from L1b — not a smaller threshold.
  • No gate can catch azimuth mis-association with two rays. A 20° azimuth error puts the fix 75 m out with a 4 m miss and a confident σ; the same error in elevation misses by 22 m and is caught. Two rays that cross produce a zero miss wherever they cross. A third post fixes it; nothing else does.
  • Cross-site association ignores track feedback. Existing predictions are strong evidence about which pairing is right. Feeding them back is a real design change, not a tweak.
  • associate handles exactly two posts. A third raises NotImplementedError rather than silently mishandling it. Past 30 contacts a post it refuses rather than slows.
  • The masking model is a screen, not a terrain model. It ignores refraction, earth curvature, partial vegetation and anything below the crest — all of which make the real picture better than predicted, which is the safe direction for a claim about what the customer cannot see.
  • The scenario models no range or detector limit. Every aircraft in clear line of sight is seen, so sensing is optimistic and the association load is pessimistic. Build 1 measured the real optical ceilings and they are not wired in yet.
Two standing rules, in force for every test above. Never add a transmit-capable wideband SDR (HackRF, PlutoSDR, bladeRF, LimeSDR) or any RF amplifier — it changes the legal posture of the whole project. And never point any part of this at a third party's drone: the target is your own aircraft, flown by your own safety pilot, with consent.
